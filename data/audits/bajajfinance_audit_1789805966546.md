# Instagram Audit Report: @bajajfinance

**Brand:** Bajaj Finance Limited  
**Audit date:** 19 September 2026 (UTC)  
**Account:** [@bajajfinance](https://www.instagram.com/bajajfinance/)  
**Account identity confirmed:** Bajaj Finance; 1,713,934 followers; 2,797 posts; bio: “For all your financial needs”  
**Sample:** 20 most recent posts published 27 August–18 September 2026, plus the exact T-2 filter for 17 September 2026  
**Data source:** Instagram connector account metadata, post list, captions, timestamps, and post insights

## Executive conclusion

The account’s strongest observable performance comes from fraud-awareness and risk-protection content, not from the conversational car-decision series. Fraud posts produced the highest lane-level interaction rate by reach at **0.696%**, while the two strongest individual posts were the investment-fraud warning (**0.969%**) and loan-settlement warning (**0.826%**). The feed is nevertheless heavily Reel-led (**19 of 20 posts; 95%**) and has only one carousel, leaving a major gap against the stated saveable finance-education strategy. Caption evidence also shows repeated weak or missing CTAs, unexplained technical terms, and several financial or offer-related claims that require proximate on-screen qualification. The recommended operating shift is to retain the high-performing warning hooks, add a disciplined educational-carousel lane, and make every post single-purpose, silent-viewable, mathematically transparent where applicable, and conversion-ready.

# SECTION 1: T-2 (48 HOURS AGO) POST DIAGNOSTIC

**T-2 date window:** 17 September 2026, 00:00–23:59 UTC.  
**T-2 post found:** [Seedhi Baat – Ep. 09 | Rapid Fire](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DdZZcjByeV5/), published **17 September 2026 at 17:31:13 UTC**. Caption opening: “No long answers. No overthinking. Bas seedhe sawaal aur instant replies.”

### 1. What Worked (Hooks, format, early reach)

- The opening is direct and avoids a greeting, satisfying the no-pleasantries rule in the caption. “No long answers. No overthinking.” is a compact pattern that communicates speed and format immediately.
- The rapid-fire format is easy to understand and creates an identifiable episodic frame. The post is a Reel, consistent with the account’s dominant format and appropriate for a short challenge concept.
- The post generated **29,028 views** and **19,894 reach**. Its visible interaction total was **94**: 64 likes, 1 comment, 14 saves, and 11 shares.
- Shares were materially stronger than comments: **11 shares** versus the sample median of **3**, indicating that the premise had some forwarding value even though it did not trigger conversation.
- The post’s interaction rate by reach was **0.473%**, calculated as `(64 + 1 + 14 + 11) / 19,894 × 100`. This is close to the 20-post sample rate of **0.515%**, so the post was not an engagement outlier in either direction.

### 2. What Did Not Work (Drop-offs, weak retention, low comments)

- The post under-reached relative to the sample: **19,894 reach** versus a 20-post average of **39,578**, approximately **50% lower**. Its **29,028 views** were approximately **57% below** the sample average of **67,866 views**.
- It produced only **94 interactions**, below the sample median of **174** and the average of **204**.
- Comment depth was weak: **1 comment**, compared with the account’s sample average of **1.2** and a median of **0**. More importantly, there is no evidence in the caption of a binary question or keyword prompt designed to convert passive viewers into commenters.
- The caption promises rapid replies but does not state a practical finance takeaway, a decision framework, or a single-action CTA such as “SAVE” or “Comment CHECKLIST.” That makes the episode more entertainment-led than utility-led.
- Actual viewer-retention drop-offs, average watch time, completion rate, and first-2.5-second visual/audio execution were not returned by the connector. Therefore, no definitive retention diagnosis can be made from the available data.

### 3. Root-Cause Analysis (Why lift or drop occurred)

The likely cause of the moderate-but-small distribution is a mismatch between a clear entertainment format and the account’s finance-learning mandate. The direct opening is useful, but “rapid fire” is a format promise rather than a viewer benefit. Without a finance dilemma, numerical contrast, or explicit decision payoff in the caption, the Reel may be less saveable and less searchable than the fraud-warning and practical-finance posts that perform better on shares and saves. The relatively high share count suggests the episode has forwarding appeal, while the low comment count suggests the content does not sufficiently invite an answer, disagreement, or self-assessment. This is a directional inference, not proof of a creative or retention failure, because visual and audio-level data were unavailable.

### 4. Strategic Adjustments (2-3 direct actionable pivots)

1. **Convert the format promise into a finance decision.** Open with one dilemma, for example: “New car or used car?” followed immediately by a numerical comparison or one decision rule. Keep the rapid-fire treatment, but make the payoff a practical finance answer.
2. **Add one low-friction CTA aligned to the episode.** End the caption and spoken/on-screen copy with a single action, such as: “Comment EMI for the breakdown” or “Save this before your next loan review.” Do not stack multiple CTAs.
3. **Instrument the first 2.5 seconds and retention loop.** Test a high-contrast visual/audio interruption, display the dilemma in 4–5-word bursts, and place a payoff or counterpoint before the midpoint. Compare 3-second hold, completion rate, shares, saves, and comments against the current episode format.

## 2.5 What is working

1. **Fraud-awareness is the clearest performance engine.** The investment-tips/guaranteed-returns warning reached **40,577** and generated **393 interactions**, including 17 shares and 24 saves. The loan-settlement warning generated **352 interactions** and **0.826%** interaction rate by reach. These posts use danger, consequence, and immediate self-protection as strong attention hooks.
2. **Specific risk scenarios outperform generic conversational framing.** The “Digital Arrest” post generated **329 interactions**, 16 shares, and 14 saves. The AI-fraud post generated 25 saves and a high views-to-reach ratio of approximately **3.09**, although that ratio should not be interpreted as unique-view completion.
3. **Some lifestyle storytelling can produce broad reach when it has a concrete scene.** The home-loan carousel reached **71,616** with **407 interactions**, 33 shares, and 24 saves. The Labrador story reached **48,948** and produced 9 comments, 19 shares, and 17 saves.
4. **The account has a recognizable episodic system.** “Seedhi Baat” and “Naye Shift Hue Ho?” create repeatable series architecture. The next step is to attach each episode to one finance decision and one action rather than removing the series structure.

## 2.6 What is not working or is underdeveloped

1. **The feed is over-dependent on Reels.** At 95% Reels and 5% carousels, the sample does not provide a robust saveable reference layer. A finance account should use carousels for formulas, checklists, document lists, comparisons, and fraud-response steps.
2. **Comment depth is structurally low.** The sample has 24 comments in total; 15 of 20 posts have zero comments. The account needs binary prompts, forced-choice questions, or keyword triggers that are directly related to the post’s decision.
3. **Car-decision content is the weakest lane by interaction rate.** Six car-choice posts average **0.381%** interaction rate by reach, versus **0.696%** for fraud-awareness. The series should not be abandoned, but it needs sharper economic stakes: total cost of ownership, EMI affordability, maintenance assumptions, fuel/charging arithmetic, or a decision checklist.
4. **Practical-finance claims are not transparently demonstrated.** “EMI,” “E20,” “CNG,” “charging infrastructure,” “up to ₹80 lakhs,” and other terms or claims appear without a visible calculation, plain-language definition, or connector-verifiable on-screen qualification.
5. **CTA governance is inconsistent.** Captions often close with narrative continuation, hashtags, or episode framing rather than one action. This conflicts with the supplied rule requiring “SAVE” or a keyword direct-response trigger.
6. **Brand linkage is uneven.** Fraud content naturally maps to trust and protection. Lifestyle stories and car conversations can earn attention but need a clear bridge to responsible borrowing, EMI planning, or financial confidence so the viewer can state what Bajaj Finance is helping them do.

## 2.7 Jargon, accessibility, and compliance review

### Accessibility and jargon

The sample includes or implies terms such as **EMI, E20 compatibility, CNG, charging infrastructure, loan settlement, professional loan, gold loan, solar finance, and digital arrest**. These should be defined in the spoken line or on-screen copy, not left only to the caption. A plain-language treatment would be: “EMI = your fixed monthly loan payment,” “E20 = petrol blended with 20% ethanol,” and “loan settlement = a negotiated closure that may affect your credit history.” Definitions should be short enough for silent mobile viewing.

### Financial claims and disclaimer risk

| Post / claim evidence | Risk flag | Required action |
|---|---|---|
| Professional Loan for CAs: “up to ₹80 lakhs” | Absolute amount and eligibility/availability implication | Require legal-approved, proximate on-screen qualification covering eligibility, terms, availability, and applicable conditions. Caption disclaimer alone is not enough evidence. |
| Digital-arrest/fraud post: “₹10,000 pay karne se pehle…” | Specific amount used in a fraud scenario | Make clear whether the amount is illustrative or based on a real case; keep the anti-fraud message and review wording for accuracy. |
| Gold Loan for higher education; solar finance; home loan; two-wheeler loan | Product benefit and access/approval implication | Verify product wording, eligibility, rate/term references, and required disclosures. Avoid implying guaranteed approval or universal suitability. |
| “Loan approval call” scenario | Approval and impersonation risk | Preserve the fraud-warning intent, but ensure the script does not imply Bajaj Finance approves loans through an unsolicited payment request. Include official-channel verification guidance. |
| “Guaranteed returns” investment warning | Fraud/compliance-sensitive promise language | Keep as a warning, define the official verification path, and ensure no creative treatment could be read as endorsing returns. |

This is a **compliance-risk flag, not a legal conclusion**. Caption evidence cannot confirm whether an on-screen disclaimer was present, readable, proximate, or repeated. Every amount, rate, benefit, eligibility statement, partner offer, approval implication, and time-bound condition should undergo legal/compliance review before publication.

## Final assessment

Bajaj Finance has a scalable audience, a recognizable episodic production system, and a demonstrably strong fraud-awareness lane. The strategic weakness is not a lack of activity; it is the absence of a consistent operating system that turns attention into saveable financial utility and measurable response. Keep the direct warning hooks, but make the broader feed more rigorous: one concept, one CTA, one worked example, one accessible definition, and one legally qualified claim treatment per post.

## Method and limitations

The audit used the exact connected account **@bajajfinance**, retrieved the 20 most recent posts available in the connector, and requested insights for all 20. The sample window is 27 August–18 September 2026 UTC. T-2 was defined as the calendar date exactly 48 hours before the audit date, 17 September 2026 UTC. Engagement rate by reach was calculated as `(likes + comments + saves + shares) / reach × 100`. “Views” are reported platform insights and are not treated as unique viewers or completed views. The connector did not provide visual frame inspection, on-screen text, audio, watch-time curves, completion rates, slide-level carousel media, or confirmed disclaimer visibility. Consequently, visual-overlay, first-2.5-second, retention, and on-screen legal-disclosure findings are explicitly marked as unverified rather than asserted as facts. Recommendations are strategic operating guidance and compliance flags, not legal advice.
