# Instagram Content Audit Report: @bajajfinance

**Audit date:** 19 September 2026  
**Account:** [@bajajfinance](https://www.instagram.com/bajajfinance/) — Bajaj Finance Limited  
**Sample:** 20 most recent posts retrieved from 27 August–18 September 2026  
**Data source:** Instagram connector account metadata, post list, and post-level insights

## Executive conclusion

@bajajfinance has a clear opportunity to turn strong awareness storytelling into a more useful finance-education system. The 20-post sample is overwhelmingly Reel-led (**19 Reels, 95%; one carousel, 5%**), with fraud-awareness content producing the strongest engagement efficiency and the entertainment-led “Seedhi Baat” lane producing weak early interaction on the calendar-date T-2 post. Sample aggregate interaction rate by reach was **0.51%**, materially below the supplied historical engagement-rate reference of **3.84%**, although the denominator and measurement window are not stated for that historical figure and therefore are not directly comparable. The main strategic gap is not a lack of attention hooks; it is the missing conversion layer: caption evidence rarely shows a single-action CTA, binary diagnostic prompt, stated arithmetic, or proximate claim qualification. The feed should preserve its accessible Hindi/Hinglish, fraud-awareness, and dilemma-based hooks while adding repeatable save/share utilities, one-topic carousels, visible silent-viewing supers, and compliance-approved decision tools.

### 1. What Worked (Hooks, format, early reach)

The Reel used a direct, low-friction promise—short answers and instant replies—rather than a greeting or a long setup. That is directionally consistent with the rule to open with the core tension or utility. The rapid-fire format is naturally scannable and has a clear entertainment device. It also generated **14 saves and 11 shares**, indicating that a subset of viewers found the format worth retaining or passing along even though the like and comment totals were low. The post reached **19,894 accounts** and generated **29,022 views**, so distribution was not zero; the weakness was conversion of attention into deeper interaction.

### 2. What Did Not Work (Drop-offs, weak retention, low comments)

The post produced only **64 likes and 1 comment**, with a **0.47% interaction rate by reach**, below the sample aggregate of 0.51%. The caption evidence frames a rapid-fire challenge but does not state a finance takeaway, a single decision framework, or a save/comment action. “No long answers” promises brevity, but it does not tell a viewer what practical problem will be solved. The connector does not expose retention curves, first-2.5-second view-through, watch time, or on-screen creative, so actual drop-off location cannot be proven. The available evidence supports a weaker diagnosis: the post likely created curiosity without enough finance utility or response friction reduction to convert it into comments and saves.

### 3. Root-Cause Analysis (Why lift or drop occurred)

The likely root cause is **format-first packaging without an explicit utility bridge**. The hook is conversational and entertainment-oriented, while the account’s strategic role is finance, credit, EMI solutions, and wealth literacy. That mismatch can lower high-intent actions even when the content is easy to watch. The post also appears episodic (“Ep. 09”), which may make it less self-contained for non-followers. Without a visible claim, formula, checklist, or binary question in the caption evidence, viewers have no clear reason to comment or save. This is an evidence-led hypothesis, not a confirmed retention finding, because visual supers and watch-time curves were unavailable.

### 4. Strategic Adjustments (2-3 direct actionable pivots)

1. **Reframe each rapid-fire episode around one finance decision.** Replace a generic challenge with a concrete opening such as “EMI low rakhni hai?” or “Loan offer asli hai?” and answer one decision framework only.
2. **Add a single-action CTA in the final frame and caption.** Example: “Comment `CHECKLIST` for the loan-call safety checklist” or “Save this before comparing EMIs.” Use one CTA, not several competing asks.
3. **Add a silent-viewing pattern interrupt and proof point in the first 2.5 seconds.** Use oversized high-contrast text in a vibrant pill, maximum 4–5 words per burst, followed by one stated assumption or arithmetic example. Measure 3-second hold, average watch time, saves per reach, shares per reach, and comments per reach in the next three episodes.

### What is working

**1. Fraud-awareness hooks are the strongest performance lane.** “Investment tips ya guaranteed returns ka waada?”, “Digital Arrest,” and loan-settlement warnings combine immediate risk, recognizable scenarios, and a concrete protective behavior. The investment-fraud post recorded **393 interactions, 24 saves, 17 shares, and 0.97% interaction rate by reach**, the highest rate in the sample. The loan-settlement post recorded **352 interactions and 0.83%**, while the Digital Arrest post recorded **329 interactions and 0.64%**. This supports retaining fraud content while connecting it to official verification and reporting steps.

**2. Dilemma-led formats are a useful attention architecture.** New vs used car, EV vs petrol, and dream car vs smart car establish tension quickly. The EV-vs-petrol post with 10 comments produced the sample’s highest comment count, suggesting that explicit disagreement or choice can invite conversation. However, the paired EV-vs-petrol episodes also show that the same topic can underperform when the framing does not add a distinct decision tool; one version produced only **80 interactions**.

**3. The single carousel materially outperformed average reach.** The “Home Loan… 80s trend” carousel reached **71,616** and generated **407 interactions**, the highest interaction total in the sample. It also produced **33 shares and 24 saves**. The result is not enough to establish causality, but it is a strong reason to test more sequential, saveable carousels rather than relying on 95% Reels.

**4. Accessible Hinglish and scenario-based storytelling are useful brand assets.** The writing is generally plain, culturally familiar, and scenario-led. The strongest examples use a concrete threat or choice rather than abstract financial jargon. The next step is not to remove this voice, but to pair it with definitions, assumptions, and a clear action.

### What is not working or is underdeveloped

**Format concentration is too high.** Nineteen of 20 posts are Reels. That supports reach experimentation but under-supplies durable reference content. The only carousel’s above-average reach and share volume make the absence of a recurring carousel system a strategic gap.

**Attention is not consistently converted into utility.** The sample average is **1.2 comments per post**, and the median is **zero**. Even the account’s strongest awareness posts generally have no comments. Add a binary diagnostic question or a keyword trigger that is directly related to the content, such as “Comment `VERIFY` for the fraud-check steps.”

**Financial claims require a visible compliance workflow.** The Professional Loan caption says “up to ₹80 lakhs”; the loan-approval scam post references paying ₹10,000; product captions mention Gold Loan, Home Loan, and Two-Wheeler Loan. The retrieved captions do not demonstrate whether required eligibility, availability, rate, duration, partner, or terms-and-conditions language is proximate and legible on-screen. This is a compliance-review flag, not a legal conclusion.

**Practical finance education lacks demonstrated arithmetic.** Car-cost content mentions petrol prices, E20, mileage, CNG, running cost, and charging infrastructure, but the connector evidence does not show formulas or stated assumptions. A stronger post would show: `monthly fuel cost = km driven × fuel price ÷ mileage`, then label every assumption and update date.

**Brand positioning is diluted by narrative episodes without a finance bridge.** Home, dog, balcony, and car-personality stories can humanize the feed, but a viewer should be able to state what Bajaj Finance helps them do after seeing the post. Each lifestyle story needs a final bridge to a financial decision, safety behavior, or wealth-literacy takeaway.

## Recommended 30-day test plan

**Week 1 — Controls and production system.** Create three approved Reel templates: fraud warning, decision framework, and product utility. Each template should reserve space for a 4–5-word burst, a qualification area, one worked assumption, and one CTA. Tag every post by lane, hook type, format, CTA type, claim presence, and whether a disclaimer was visible in the creative.

**Weeks 2–3 — Controlled creative tests.** Run matched tests across four pairs: warning hook versus question hook; Reel versus carousel; generic episode label versus specific decision promise; and narrative ending versus keyword CTA. Keep topic, audience, and publishing window as comparable as practical. Do not change multiple variables in a single test.

**Week 4 — Scale winners and retire weak patterns.** Promote the two highest-performing structures based on saves per reach, shares per reach, comments per reach, and completion rate—not likes alone. A post should be considered a utility winner when it earns both above-baseline reach efficiency and a meaningful save/share signal. Review all financial claims with compliance before reuse.

**Measurement dashboard.** Track reach, views, 3-second hold, average watch time, completion rate, likes, comments, saves, shares, reported interactions, interaction rate by reach, saves per reach, shares per reach, CTA response rate, and claim-review status. Keep the formula consistent: interaction rate by reach equals reported interactions divided by reach multiplied by 100.

## Method and limitations

The connector returned the exact requested username, account metadata, 20 recent posts, and post-level insights for every sampled post. The sample window is based on the returned timestamps and is not intended as a statistically representative historical study. Caption text and connector metadata were treated as evidence. Visual supers, color contrast, font size, audio, first-2.5-second pattern interrupts, slide-by-slide carousel triggers, watch-time curves, and retention drop-offs were **not available** through the retrieved fields; they are therefore marked not verifiable rather than assumed absent. A caption that lacks a disclaimer is not proof that the creative lacks an on-screen disclaimer. Financial claims are flagged for compliance review, not declared unlawful or non-compliant. The supplied 3.84% historical engagement rate is not directly comparable to this report’s reach-based sample rate without a matching denominator and measurement definition.
