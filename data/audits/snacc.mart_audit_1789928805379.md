## Executive conclusion

@snacc.mart has a clear local-snacking identity and a recognizable content voice built around humor, relatable snack moments, customer warmth, and Bengaluru/Electronic City discovery. The strongest public signals are on the June 8 customer-love Reel (100 likes, 3 comments) and the June 29 “forgot something” Reel (40 likes, 5 comments), while the July 31 Reel has 26 likes and 5 comments. The finance-specific rules supplied in the brief are not category-fit for a snack retailer; they should be translated into snack-relevant equivalents rather than copied literally. ### 1. What Worked (Hooks, format, early reach)

No T-2 post was found, so there is no T-2 creative or early-reach performance to diagnose. ### 2. What Did Not Work (Drop-offs, weak retention, low comments)

The operational issue visible around T-2 is a posting gap: the latest publicly visible post was 51 days before the audit date. That gap makes it impossible to sustain a reliable 2-Reel-plus-1-Carousel weekly cadence.

### 3. Root-Cause Analysis (Why lift or drop occurred)

The absence of a T-2 post is best explained by a cadence and content-system gap, not by a measured creative failure. The public feed is clustered around 4–10 June, then moves to 29 June, 3 July, and 31 July, with no visible August or 18 September activity. Without native Insights, the causes of any reach or retention movement cannot be separated into hook strength, distribution, audience size, watch time, or creative quality.

### 4. Strategic Adjustments (2-3 direct actionable pivots)

1. **Re-establish a predictable weekly floor:** publish two Reels and one saveable Carousel every week for four consecutive weeks, using a fixed Monday/Wednesday/Saturday or Tuesday/Thursday/Sunday rhythm.
2. **Convert every Reel into a next action:** end with one specific prompt such as “Comment CHAKLI for today’s pairing,” “Save this for your evening snack run,” or “DM MENU for the current menu.” Track comments, DMs, saves, and profile visits rather than likes alone.
3. **Use a 48-hour diagnostic checkpoint:** at 2 hours, 24 hours, and 48 hours record reach, 3-second plays, average watch time, completion rate, saves, shares, comments, profile visits, and follows. Do not label a post a winner or loser from likes alone.

### Format mix and cadence finding

The inspected sample is **80% Reels and 20% static photos**, with **0% publicly identified Carousels**. This supports discovery and personality, but it does not support a reliable saveable-reference layer. The strongest strategic correction is not to replace Reels; it is to add one practical Carousel each week: “Murukku vs Chakli,” “3 snack boxes for office sharing,” “What to pair with chai,” or “Electronic City snack-run checklist.”

### Accessibility and compliance review

The inspected captions are generally plain-language and do not show unexplained technical jargon. The account should keep this strength in on-screen text by using short, high-contrast phrases and subtitles for silent viewing. Visual accessibility cannot be verified from public caption extraction, so on-screen typography, contrast, text-safe margins, audio clarity, and alt-text practices remain open checks.

No observed post contained a financial amount, rate, approval promise, return, or partner offer, so the finance disclaimer rule was not triggered. For this snack category, the equivalent risk areas are **price and discount claims, “healthy” or “light” claims, freshness/safety claims, delivery timing, and availability statements**. These should be accurate, time-bounded where necessary, and supported by a proximate qualification when the claim has conditions.

## Recommended 30-day test plan

**Week 1:** Publish one humor Reel, one product-proof Reel, and one comparison Carousel. Test two CTA families: “SAVE” versus a keyword DM. Keep the visual style consistent and record baseline reach, plays, average watch time, completion, saves, shares, comments, profile visits, and DMs at 2, 24, and 48 hours.

**Week 2:** Test hook type rather than topic: a binary choice (“Murukku or Chakli?”) against a curiosity premise (“The snack most people pick first”). Keep the offer, location, and CTA constant so the hook is the main variable.

**Week 3:** Publish a local utility Carousel and a Reel showing the store/product experience. Test whether practical content generates more saves and profile actions than entertainment alone.

**Week 4:** Repeat the winning hook and CTA combination, then compare medians rather than a single viral outlier. Continue the cadence only if the content system is sustainable for the operator.

### Recommended measurement definitions

Use **interaction rate by reach** when native reach is available: `(likes + comments + saves + shares) / reach × 100`. Track **save rate** as saves/reach, **share rate** as shares/reach, **CTA conversion** as CTA actions/reach, and **completion rate** as completed plays/plays. Do not use likes divided by followers as a substitute for reach-based engagement rate.

## Final assessment

Snacc Mart does not need a finance-content playbook; it needs the same disciplined operating principles translated into food retail: a dependable cadence, one clear snack idea per post, an immediate visual hook, practical saveable education, and one trackable action. Preserve the warmth and humor that already make the brand locally recognizable, but connect each piece to a product choice, visit reason, or response mechanism.
